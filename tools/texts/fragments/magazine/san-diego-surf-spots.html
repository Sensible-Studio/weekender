San Diego County has more than a hundred kilometres of coast facing the open Pacific, and somewhere along it a wave is working on almost any day of the year. Winter brings the larger swells from the north-west, summer the gentler ones from the south, and the water never gets truly cold. I have lived here for twelve years and still argue with friends about where to go. Here are six breaks, from south to north, and who each one suits.

Ocean Beach

The pier and the jetty at the end of the main street shape sandbars that can be very good and are rarely empty. The town behind the beach has kept its scruffy charm: surf shops, dogs, a farmers' market on Wednesdays. The waves are fast and the regulars know them well, so this is one for confident surfers. Everyone else can watch from the pier with a coffee.

Tourmaline

At the north end of Pacific Beach a car park drops straight onto a wide, slow, forgiving wave. This is the home of the longboard in San Diego. On a small morning there may be sixty people out, aged from nine to eighty, and room for all of them. If you are in your first year of surfing, come here, and take the advice that the older riders will certainly offer.

Windansea

A reef in a quiet part of La Jolla, with a palm-thatched shack on the rocks that has stood, in one form or another, since the 1940s. The wave is short, hollow and powerful, and it breaks close to stone. It also has the most protective locals on this list. Visit, by all means, and sit on the sand to watch a good swell. Paddle out only if you know exactly what you are doing.

La Jolla Shores

A long, sandy, gently sloping beach where most of the city's surf schools give their lessons. The waves are small and soft for much of the year, there are lifeguards and showers, and leopard sharks, which are harmless, gather in the shallows in late summer. For a first day on a board there is no better place in the county.

Black's Beach

Below the cliffs of Torrey Pines a deep underwater canyon points straight at the shore and focuses the swell into the heaviest beach break in Southern California. Getting there means a steep walk down a path that is hard work on the way back up with a board. On a big winter day it is a wave for experts only, and one of the great sights of the coast from the top of the cliff.

Swami's

In Encinitas, half an hour north, a long staircase leads down from a small park to a right-hand point that comes alive on winter swells. The name comes from the gold-domed meditation gardens on the bluff above. The line-up is crowded and skilled, but the wave is long enough to share. Afterwards walk into town for fish tacos, which is how every surf in this part of the world ought to end.
