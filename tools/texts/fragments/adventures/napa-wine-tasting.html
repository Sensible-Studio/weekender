A slow day in the Napa Valley

The Napa Valley is fifty kilometres of vineyards an hour north of San Francisco, and its famous names can be crowded and formal. We go instead to three small, family-run wineries where the person pouring is often the person who made the wine. A driver takes care of the road, so everyone can taste.

== itinerary

Morning: sparkling wine

We leave the city at nine and start, as one should, with bubbles: a small producer on the cooler southern edge of the valley, with a walk through the vines and a look at the cellar.

Midday: a hillside estate

Up a winding road on the eastern side, a family has farmed the same slope for three generations. We taste their Cabernet from the barrel and from the bottle, and have a picnic lunch under the oaks with the valley below.

Afternoon: the old vines

The last visit is to a grower who works Zinfandel vines planted a century ago. A final tasting in the garden, and we are back in San Francisco by six.

== gearList
  • Shoes for walking on gravel and between vines
  • A sun hat, and a layer for the cool cellars
  • A photo ID showing that you are 21 or older
