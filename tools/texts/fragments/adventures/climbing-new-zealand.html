Two days on South Island rock

The mountains of New Zealand's South Island offer solid rock, long views and very few people. This weekend is for climbers who have learned indoors, or have climbed a little outside, and want to move with confidence on real rock.

The group is never larger than three for each guide, so the days adapt to you.

  • Day one: technique and rope work on single-pitch routes
  • Day two: a multi-pitch climb to a summit
== itinerary

Day 1: Finding your feet

We meet in town at eight and drive half an hour to a crag above the lake. The morning is spent on easy routes, working on footwork and balance. After lunch at the foot of the cliff we practise belaying, building anchors and abseiling, until everyone can do each of them without help. Dinner is at the lodge, and the evening ends with a look at the map for the next day.

Day 2: The long route

An early start and an hour's walk lead to the foot of a ridge of six pitches. The climbing is easy and the position is not: by the third pitch the valley is a long way below. We reach the top around midday, eat with the Southern Alps in front of us, and walk down a path on the far side. We are back in town by five.

== gearList

Ropes, harness, helmet and rock shoes are provided; bring your own if you prefer them.

Please bring walking shoes, warm and waterproof layers, sunglasses, sunscreen, a small rucksack and two litres of water.
