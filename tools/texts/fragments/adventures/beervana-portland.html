A day among Portland's small breweries

Portland has more breweries than any city of its size, most of them small, independent and within walking distance of each other. This is a day on foot between four of them, in the company of a guide who has brewed for a living.

You will taste around a dozen beers in small glasses, see how they are made and eat well along the way. It is a day for the curious, and you do not need to know anything about beer to enjoy it.

== itinerary

We meet at eleven in the inner east side, a district of old warehouses that brewers moved into twenty years ago.

Morning: how beer is made

The first stop is a working brewhouse. Among the tanks we look at malt, hops, yeast and water, smell and taste each of them, and follow a batch from grain to glass. The tasting that follows covers the main families of beer, from a pale lager to a dark stout.

Lunch: food and beer together

A short walk brings us to a brewery with a kitchen. The cook pairs four small plates with four beers and explains each choice.

Afternoon: the experiments

The last two stops are tiny. One ages its beers in wine barrels, the other brews with fruit from farms nearby. We finish by five, close to a tram stop.

== gearList
  • Comfortable shoes: we walk about four kilometres in all
  • A photo ID showing that you are 21 or older
  • A rain jacket, in any season
