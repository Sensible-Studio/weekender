Tuscany at the speed of a bicycle

A car is too fast for Tuscany. On a bike you smell the cypress and the wood smoke, you hear the bells, and you arrive in each hill town hungry enough to do justice to lunch. We ride quiet lanes and the white gravel roads between vineyards, between 40 and 60 kilometres a day, and sleep in farmhouses and small family hotels.

== itinerary

Day 1: The Chianti hills

From a village among the vineyards we ride south over rolling hills to Siena, entering the city through one of its medieval gates in time for an evening walk to the square.

Day 2: Clay hills

South of Siena the land opens into bare, pale ridges with a single farmhouse on each. A long gravel section leads to an abbey among the cypresses, and to dinner at a farm that makes its own pecorino.

Day 3: Val d'Orcia

The postcard day: a winding avenue of cypresses, a hot spring where you can soak tired legs, and a climb to a hill town known for its red wine.

Day 4: Back through the vines

A shorter ride on quiet roads to the last lunch, long and unhurried, before the transfer to Florence.

== gearList

The weather in the hills changes during the day, so think in layers.

  • Helmet and cycling gloves
  • Padded shorts and two jerseys
  • A light rain jacket
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen
  • Shoes you can also walk in
  • Light clothes for the evenings
